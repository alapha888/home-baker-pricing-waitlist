Price the same bake three ways: what you actually keep on Etsy, on Instagram, and at the market stall

This is a planned spreadsheet (Excel + Google Sheets) for home bakers and cottage-food sellers who sell through more than one channel. Recipe-cost calculators — including the ones already selling on Etsy — answer one question: what does this recipe cost to make? They stop there. But the same box of cookies keeps a different amount on each channel: Etsy takes its transaction, listing and payment fees; a market day adds a stall fee that has to be spread over whatever you actually sell; packaging differs between a shipped order and a market bag; and unsold perishable stock at the end of a market day is a cost the shipped channel never has. This sheet, if built, would put recipe cost, packaging, spoilage and each channel's fee stack in one place, so the estimated margin per channel — after known fees and per-order variable costs, before your own labor, rent and taxes — sits side by side for the same product. It has not been built. This page answers one question only: is the per-channel layer worth building, at an intended price of $14.99 one-time?

Why validate before building

  • Baking cost spreadsheets are an established paid category on Etsy: at least five comparable listings were found first-hand, priced around $6.99–$9.99, recipe-cost focused. The category sells. What is not established is whether pricing per channel — rather than per recipe — matters enough for anyone to pay a premium over those listings. That is the untested hypothesis this page exists to test.
  • The intended price here, $14.99, sits deliberately above the $6.99–$9.99 recipe-only band. The premium has to be earned by the channel layer: fee presets per channel that you can update when a platform changes its rates, stall-fee allocation per market day, and spoilage accounting for perishable stock. If signups say the recipe number is all they need, the cheaper existing listings already do the job and this idea gets dropped.
  • Free alternatives are real: a generic spreadsheet template or an AI chat can cost out a single recipe. Neither keeps a per-channel margin table for your whole product line that recalculates when an ingredient price or a platform fee changes. When you sign up, please say plainly whether that is a problem you actually have.

Planned modules (not built yet)

  • Recipe cost sheet: ingredients by weight/unit, cost per batch and per item
  • Packaging per order type: shipping box vs. market bag vs. gift packaging, costed per order
  • Channel fee stacks: Etsy (transaction + listing + payment processing), other platforms, and direct/Instagram orders (payment fees only), each editable when rates change
  • Market day sheet: stall fee and day costs allocated over items actually sold
  • Spoilage and unsold stock: end-of-day markdown/write-off rate applied to perishable lines
  • Per-channel margin table: the same product's estimated margin on every channel, side by side, with three pricing methods compared (cost-plus, target margin, market benchmark)

Estimates from this kind of sheet are a floor for your pricing decisions, not a profit figure: your labor, rent, equipment and taxes sit outside it, and the sheet would say so in plain text. What actually gets built — and which modules come first — will be decided by what signups say.

Who this is for

  • Home bakers and cottage-food sellers who sell the same products through two or more channels and price mainly by feel
  • Market vendors who have never spread the stall fee over a day's actual sales, or costed unsold stock
  • If you sell through one channel only, or a recipe-cost calculator already answers your question, you do not need this — use the cheaper existing tools

FAQ

Can I buy it now?
No. It has not been built. This page is the whole test.

Does signing up commit me to buying?
No. Signup only means "tell me if it gets built." You decide when you see the finished product.

How is this different from the baking calculators already on Etsy?
Those price a recipe. This one, if the hypothesis holds, prices a recipe per channel: the same item's estimated margin after Etsy's fee stack, after a market day's stall fee and unsold stock, and after direct-order payment fees, in one table. If that difference is not worth anything to sellers, this page will show it and the idea closes.

Does the margin number include my labor?
No — and it should not pretend to. The planned table covers ingredients, packaging, spoilage and channel fees. Labor, rent, equipment and taxes are yours to add on top; the sheet would label the margin accordingly.
